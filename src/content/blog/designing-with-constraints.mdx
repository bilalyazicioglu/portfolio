title: "Designing With Constraints"
summary: "Why the most interesting design decisions I've made came from a limitation, not a blank canvas."
date: "2026-06-02"

---

Every project I've enjoyed working on had one thing in common: a real constraint early on that ruled out the obvious solution.

A blank canvas is paralyzing. Ten possible layouts all look equally plausible, and without a reason to prefer one over another, you end up bikeshedding. A constraint — a tight deadline, an odd screen size, a single accent color — collapses that space fast.

## Constraints I keep coming back to

- **One typeface family, two weights.** Forces hierarchy through size and spacing instead of font-hopping.
- **No more than one accent color.** Everything else is ink, paper, and grays.
- **A grid you can't break.** Even "breaking the grid" reads better when there was a grid to break.

## The trap

The failure mode is treating constraints as decoration instead of structure. A grid drawn on top of a layout that ignores it isn't a constraint, it's a lie. The constraint has to change what you build, not just how it looks.

Next time a project feels aimless, I don't reach for more inspiration — I reach for a rule to cut the options down.
